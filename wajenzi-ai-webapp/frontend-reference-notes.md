# Market-Ready Frontend Direction

The authenticated and public front end is being replaced to follow the user-provided wajenzi.ai reference direction. The visual language uses a deep charcoal construction-network surface, teal action accents, light operational canvases, generous editorial display typography, restrained cards, and responsive mobile-first controls.

## Required user journey

The public entry should offer a conversational project/sourcing prompt, canonical-product marketplace discovery, a clear construction operating-system proposition, and role entry points. Canonical products may be public. Supplier price, stock, tax, facility, quotation, delivery, and fulfilment claims remain commercial records and must load only in their applicable authorized workflows, with verification and demonstration labels retained.

After authentication, the system resolves organization membership, permitted role/persona, project scope, and workspace context before routing to the role workspace. A user can have multiple organizations and roles. Roles must never be inferred solely from browser state.

## Dashboard and subdomain direction

All role experiences share a Wajenzi mark, marketplace access, search, AI, notifications, messages, tasks, approvals, profile, organization switcher, role switcher, project switcher, help, and settings. Navigation and the opening dashboard change by permitted persona. Requested production host patterns include `admin.wajenzi.ai`, supplier, contractor, manufacturer, client, architect, engineer, QS, project-manager, logistics, financier, institution, developer, and operations role hosts. The app must keep a safe non-subdomain preview fallback.

## Workflow boundaries

The desired contractor path spans project setup, documents, BOQ/WBS, estimate, procurement, RFQ, supplier quotation, comparison, approval, purchase order, delivery, receipt, inventory, work, progress, payment, variations, handover, and closeout. The supplier path spans product data, catalogue, pricing, stock, invited RFQs, quotations, orders, delivery, invoice, and payment. Only the already governed services may be represented as live. All remaining operational, delivery, receipt, inventory, POS, invoice, and financial transitions must retain guided or planned labels until their records, authorization, evidence, audit, and event contracts exist.
